Theoretical Q&A
Q1: Explain what hardness in water means? Additionally, identify and describe the two types of hardness mentioned.
Definition: Water hardness is the characteristic which prevents the lathering of soap or the soap-consuming capacity of water.
Types:
  • Temporary Hardness: Caused by bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium. Can be removed by boiling.
  • Permanent Hardness: Caused by chlorides and sulphates of calcium and magnesium. Cannot be removed by boiling.
Q2: List and explain the units of hardness used in water quality assessment? Demonstrate the application of the EDTA method in determining water hardness.
Units: ppm, mg/lit, Degree Clarke (°Cl), and Degree French (°Fr).
EDTA Method: A complexometric titration technique using standard EDTA solution and EBT indicator at pH 9-10 to determine total hardness.
Q3: Provide a detailed procedure, including the specific reagents used and chemical reactions.
Reagents: Standard EDTA, Buffer solution (NH4Cl + NH4OH), EBT indicator, Standard CaCl2.
Reactions:
Ca2+ + EBT → [Ca-EBT] (Wine-red complex)
[Ca-EBT] + EDTA → [Ca-EDTA] (Stable, colorless) + EBT (Blue color release)
Q4: Simplify Complexometric titration. Illustrate how water hardness is measured using the EDTA method.
Complexometric titration involves forming coordination complexes. Water sample containing Ca2+ and Mg2+ is buffered and titrated with EDTA until the indicator turns from wine-red to blue.
Q5: Interpret the concept of water hardness. Execute the application of the Zeolite Method in softening water.
Zeolite (Na2O·Al2O3·xSiO2·yH2O) replaces hard water calcium and magnesium ions with sodium ions. Exhausted zeolite is regenerated using 5-10% brine (NaCl) solution.
Q6: Explain how De-ionization/demineralization treats water and how Ion exchange helps.
Removes all dissolved minerals using Cation exchange resin (replaces cations with H+) and Anion exchange resin (replaces anions with OH-), followed by a degasifier for CO2 removal.
Q7: Explain briefly what scale and sludge formation are in water systems and why they occur.
Sludge: Loose, slimy precipitate formed at cooler zones.
Scale: Hard, adherent crust formed on boiler walls due to salt decomposition at high temperatures.
Q8: Describe 'Caustic Embrittlement' in an easy way. Why is it important?
Boiler metal cracking due to localized accumulation of caustic NaOH formed from Na2CO3 hydrolysis. Crucial to prevent structural boiler failures.
Q9: Provide a simple explanation of sustainability with an example.
Meeting present needs without compromising future generations across environmental, economic, and social domains. Example: Industrial wastewater recycling.
Solved Numerical Problems
Problem 1: 100 ml water sample requires 12.7 ml of 0.01 M EDTA. Calculate hardness.
Total Hardness = (12.7 / 100) × 0.01 × 105 = 127 ppm
Problem 2: 100 ml water sample requires 15.6 ml EDTA before boiling and 10.7 ml after boiling. Calculate carbonate and non-carbonate hardness.
Total Hardness = (15.6 / 100) × 0.01 × 105 = 156 ppm
Permanent Hardness = (10.7 / 100) × 0.01 × 105 = 107 ppm
Carbonate Hardness = 156 - 107 = 49 ppm
Problem 3: 0.5 gm CaCO3 dissolved in 500 ml. 50 ml required 48 ml EDTA. 25 ml hard water required 7.5 ml EDTA and after boiling 5 ml. Calculate hardness.
Molarity of CaCO3 = 0.01 M. EDTA Molarity = 0.0104 M.
Total Hardness = (7.5 / 25) × 0.0104 × 100 × 1000 = 312.5 ppm
Permanent Hardness = 208 ppm
Temporary Hardness = 312.5 - 208 = 104.5 ppm
Problem 4: 0.8 gm ZnSO4 dissolved to 250 ml. 25 ml required 14.3 ml EDTA. 50 ml water sample required 8.6 ml EDTA. Calculate hardness.
Molarity of ZnSO4 = 0.0198 M → EDTA Molarity = 0.0347 M.
Total Hardness = (8.6 / 50) × 0.0347 × 100 × 1000 = 596 ppm
